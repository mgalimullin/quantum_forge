# Fallout(TV series)

***Fallout*** is a post-apocalyptic drama television series based on the [video game series](Fallout%20series.md "Fallout series") of the same name. While sharing the [setting](Fallout%20setting.md "Fallout setting") of the franchise's mainline video games, the plot is that of an original story rather than attempting to retell one from previous entries. Licensed to Amazon Studios, the series was developed by [Kilter Films](Kilter%20Films.md "Kilter Films"), in association with [Bethesda Game Studios](Bethesda%20Softworks.md "Bethesda Softworks") and [Bethesda Softworks](Bethesda%20Softworks.md "Bethesda Softworks"). Executive producers include [Jonathan Nolan](Jonathan%20Nolan.md "Jonathan Nolan"), [Lisa Joy](Lisa%20Joy.md "Lisa Joy"), and [Athena Wickham](Athena%20Wickham.md "Athena Wickham") for Kilter Films, [Todd Howard](Todd%20Howard.md "Todd Howard") for Bethesda Game Studios, and [James Altman](James%20Altman.md "James Altman") for Bethesda Softworks.

*Fallout* entered production in mid-2022, with Jonathan Nolan directing the first three episodes, as well as [Geneva Robertson-Dworet](Geneva%20Robertson-Dworet.md "Geneva Robertson-Dworet") and [Graham Wagner](Graham%20Wagner.md "Graham Wagner") serving as showrunners. Production and filming for the first season took place across New York, New Jersey, Utah and the ghost town of Kolmanskop in the country of Namibia.

The series premiered on April 10, 2024. It was renewed for a [second season](Fallout%20TV%20series%20season%202.md "Fallout TV series season 2"), which began filming in November 2024 in Los Angeles under the codename *Dakota*, and entered post-production circa May 8, 2025. On May 12, 2025, it was announced that season 2 would release in December 2025, and that the series had also been renewed for a [third season](Fallout%20TV%20series%20season%203.md "Fallout TV series season 3"). Season 2 premiered on December 16, 2025, and is currently to release one episode weekly until February 4, 2026.

The *Fallout* TV series is not to be confused with *[Fallout: A Post Nuclear Role Playing Game](Fallout.md "Fallout")* (identified on this wiki as *Fallout* for short), the 1997 first entry of the *Fallout* franchise. Despite belonging to separate mediums, both installments are set in the same region of post-apocalyptic California, with the town of [Shady Sands](Shady%20Sands.md "Shady Sands") appearing in both media.

## Contents

## 

The story of *Fallout* is set in [2296](/wiki/Timeline#2296 "Timeline"), 219 years after the global [nuclear](Nuclear%20weapons.md "Nuclear weapons") holocaust known as the [Great War](Great%20War.md "Great War"). During this apocalyptic event, some managed to survive in [Vaults](Vault.md "Vault"), massive fallout shelters constructed by the [Vault-Tec Corporation](Vault-Tec%20Corporation.md "Vault-Tec Corporation") across the [United States](United%20States%20of%20America.md "United States of America"), while leaving the rest outside to suffer and struggle for survival in the [wasteland](Wasteland.md "Wasteland").

[Lucy MacLean](Lucy%20MacLean.md "Lucy MacLean") is a resident of one such Vault: [Vault 33](Vault%2033.md "Vault 33"), located in the ruined city of [Los Angeles](Boneyard.md "Boneyard"). She has spent her life underground and is currently applying to have a husband from [Vault 32](Vault%2032.md "Vault 32") in an arranged marriage. After a tragic event involving a woman named [Lee Moldaver](Lee%20Moldaver.md "Lee Moldaver"), Lucy decides to leave for the surface on a mission to save her father [Hank](Hank%20MacLean.md "Hank MacLean").

As she becomes accustomed to the dangers of the wasteland, she encounters other characters including [Maximus](Maximus.md "Maximus"), a young soldier in the [Brotherhood of Steel](Brotherhood%20of%20Steel.md "Brotherhood of Steel"), as well as [the Ghoul](The%20Ghoul.md "The Ghoul"), a [ghoulified](Ghoul.md "Ghoul") [bounty hunter](Bounty%20hunter.md "Bounty hunter"). The protagonists' paths converge as they chase an artifact from an enigmatic researcher named [Siggi Wilzig](Siggi%20Wilzig.md "Siggi Wilzig") which has the potential to reshape the power dynamic of the entire wasteland. Other perspectives include that of pre-War Hollywood star, Cooper Howard, the man who became the Ghoul; as well as the continued story of Vault 33's dwellers after Lucy leaves on her journey, shown through the perspective of her brother [Norm MacLean](Norm%20MacLean.md "Norm MacLean").

Other details about the series include [Filly](Filly.md "Filly"), a junkyard-like settlement in the greater Los Angeles area; the *[Prydwen](The%20Prydwen.md "The Prydwen")*, an airship built and used by the Brotherhood; as well as [Vault-Tec Corporation](Vault-Tec%20Corporation.md "Vault-Tec Corporation") mascot [Vault Boy](Vault%20Boy.md "Vault Boy") being given a backstory. The series features four Vaults ([4](Vault%204.md "Vault 4"), [31](Vault%2031.md "Vault 31"), 32, and 33), the latter three are interconnected via exchanging resources.

### 

Similar to the video games, *Fallout* features sociopolitical commentary and was intended to challenge the idea of black-and-white morality. A major goal was to get away from the binary thinking of people as being simply good or evil.

One of the story's themes is about exploring America as a place of haves and have-nots, and how being wealthy and never having to struggle for basic amenities such as food can drastically alter someone's perspective and opinions. *Fallout* explores Lucy's collision with the hard reality of other people’s experiences and what happened to the people who had no Vault and were left behind to die. Director Nolan compares this to real-life America, saying the wealth gap division has intensified over the last decades. Lucy's status as a Vault Dweller is mocked by characters such as [Ma June](Ma%20June.md "Ma June") and the Ghoul who view her as privileged.

Another intended theme regarding the Vaults is them being used as a metaphor about the hardships of immigration, particularly countries with open/closed border policies, and what excluding certain people could mean. Other themes include the morality of the death penalty, how people turn to religion in times of grief, how civilizations and factions are not immortal, how people's individual freedoms and human rights may be under attack, and the importance of exercising caution when dealing with strangers. Another theme is how people may not be what they seem, with Lucy discovering a major revelation about someone she trusted, while also jumping to negative conclusions about a particular group who seem evil and sinister, but she winds up misjudging their intentions.

The show was criticized by certain people for having one of the four protagonists be a woman, resulting in Ella Purnell criticizing this idea, saying the *Fallout* games are inherently political and that analyzing society and its different groups is part of the DNA of *Fallout*. Purnell said she loves seeing women in survival dramas and seeing the brutality and violence women are capable of when they are hungry and in survival mode, and that it breaks stereotypes of what women have to be portrayed as in media, commenting, "You're not this together, pretty picture that films originally depicted women as."

### 

According to [Todd Howard](Todd%20Howard.md "Todd Howard"), the events of the *Fallout* TV series are considered part of the official *Fallout* canon, as he states, "We view what's happening in the show as canon." [Bethesda Softworks](Bethesda%20Softworks.md "Bethesda Softworks") has had some amount of oversight to ensure that the scripts fit within the existing world of *Fallout* and the series' previous titles. Howard and other show creators view the TV series as a mainline series entry in its own right, akin to *[Fallout 5](Fallout%205.md "Fallout 5")*.

The idea for a TV series adaptation of *Fallout* were first discussed between Todd Howard and Jonathan Nolan in 2019. According to Nolan, Howard had rejected various TV adaptation offers in the past. Nolan had played *[Fallout 3](Fallout%203.md "Fallout 3")* very extensively and was "very taken" with the *Fallout* setting. They found it easy to work together during their initial meeting and continued discussions over the coming months.

The rights to the TV series adaptation of *Fallout* were purchased by Amazon in 2020 following months of negotiation. A teaser for the series was released on July 2, 2020, revealing Amazon and Kilter Films' involvement. Following the announcement, the Kilter team stated, "*Fallout* is one of the greatest game series of all time. Each chapter of this insanely imaginative story has cost us countless hours we could have spent with family and friends. So we're incredibly excited to partner with Todd Howard and the rest of the brilliant lunatics at Bethesda to bring this massive, subversive, and darkly funny universe to life with Amazon Studios."

Albert Cheng, chief operating officer and co-head of television at Amazon Studios, said, "*Fallout* is an iconic global franchise, with legions of fans worldwide and a rich, deeply compelling storyline that powers it. And Jonah and Lisa are the perfect storytellers to bring this series to life. We’re thrilled to join with Bethesda to bring *Fallout* to television." Todd Howard stated that "over the last decade, we looked at many ways to bring *Fallout* to the screen, but it was clear from the moment I first spoke with Jonah and Lisa a few years ago, that they and the team at Kilter were the ones to do it right. We're enormous fans of their work and couldn't be more excited to work with them and Amazon Studios."

In an August 2021 interview, Lisa Joy described the upcoming series as "a gonzo, crazy, funny, adventure, and mindfuck like none you’ve ever seen before. It’s pretty cool." In a November 2021 Reddit AMA, Todd Howard confirmed that the series was still in motion, stating: "[It's] moving ahead. [I'm] really excited to be working with Jonah, Lisa, and Amazon on it."

In January 2022, it was announced that screenwriters [Geneva Robertson-Dworet](Geneva%20Robertson-Dworet.md "Geneva Robertson-Dworet") and [Graham Wagner](Graham%20Wagner.md "Graham Wagner") would serve as showrunners for the series, which would be entering production that same year.

### 

In an August 2020 interview, Bethesda design director [Emil Pagliarulo](Emil%20Pagliarulo.md "Emil Pagliarulo") stated that Bethesda set up lore information and guidelines for the series' writers, but afterward the writers "go off and do their things" as they are the experts. However, Howard also advised the showrunners to avoid certain elements that would be in *[Fallout 5](Fallout%205.md "Fallout 5")*.

It also revealed the casting of [Michael John Benzaia](Michael%20John%20Benzaia.md "Michael John Benzaia") and [Michael Emerson](Michael%20Emerson.md "Michael Emerson"), the latter specifically by way of a stuntman.

#### 

Production on the series began in mid-2022. In July 2022, Goggins confirmed via Instagram that filming had commenced on the series in New York, with a behind-the-scenes photo of himself at a makeup trailer, with "GHOUL" written on a sign behind him. That same month, several photos began to leak from the series' principal photography, including that of a set repurposing a Staten Island supermarket for a [Super-Duper Mart](Super-Duper%20Mart%20company.md "Super-Duper Mart (company)"). As filming continued, more set images were leaked in January 2023, featuring a [Red Rocket](Red%20Rocket.md "Red Rocket") [station](Red%20Rocket%20TV%20series.md "Red Rocket (TV series)") constructed for a set in Nyack, New York. Filming wrapped by February 2023. Another filming location was the Skeleton Coast of Namibia (including in particular the ghost town [Kolmanskop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolmanskop "wikipedia:Kolmanskop")), a personal favorite of Jonathan Nolan, which was used to depict various locations in the wasteland around Los Angeles. This location was actually what inspired the producers and writers to choose Los Angeles as the setting of the *Fallout* show; the earliest drafts for the show had it set in Colorado instead, according to production designer Howard Cummings, who stated that during a scouting trip to an abandoned mining town along the Skeleton Coast, he had a daydream of the [Santa Monica Pier](Santa%20Monica%20Pier.md "Santa Monica Pier") extending out from the shoreline, which contributed to him suggesting the change of setting to Los Angeles.

Scenes were also filmed in the towns of [Cortlandt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cortlandt,_New_York "wikipedia:Cortlandt, New York") and [Briarcliff Manor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Briarcliff_Manor,_New_York "wikipedia:Briarcliff Manor, New York") in Westchester County, New York, the latter of which specifically was used for the opening scene of "[The End](The%20End%20episode.md "The End (episode)")." Other scenes were filmed in Utah, including the Brotherhood of Steel's base, which was shot at [Wendover Airfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wendover_Airport "wikipedia:Wendover Airport"), a former U.S. Air Force base. The only scenes filmed in Southern California for Season 1 were several shots of second-unit drone footage focused on Griffith Observatory, although the physical scenes with the observatory were instead filmed at Long Island, New York.

At Jonathan Nolan's direction, the series was shot with a film camera in order to achieve a Technicolor look, befitting the 1950s style. The majority of the props seen in the series are real and not CGI; for example, all of the [T-60 power armor suits](T-60%20power%20armor%20TV%20series.md "T-60 power armor (TV series)") were real full-body suits created by , the company that produced the Iron Man suits for the eponymous movie *Iron Man*, and the [Pip-Boys](Pip-Boy%203000%20Mark%20V.md "Pip-Boy 3000 Mark V") were created as frames used to hold iPhones that used a special app to appear like a Pip-Boy screen. Many other scenery props were also real; for example, much of the furniture and decorations in Vault 33 were real-world products purchased from various vendors.

The production team made heavy use of physically recreated in-game assets, such as posters, paintings, and even architecture. This was made possible due to the production crew working directly with Bethesda Game Studios and being given access to their 3D models and renderings (primarily from *[Fallout 76](Fallout%2076.md "Fallout 76")*), which Howard Cummings and assistants used to physically reproduce said objects, including Vault room pieces, with a focus on authenticity to the games. Cummings also cites referencing various fan projects and wikis to inform the process.

Visual effects for the series were also done using a mixture of practical effects and CGI. The yao guai in "The Target" was made using CGI on top of a stunt performer wearing a costume; other agents such as the gulper in "The Head" or Snip Snip in "The Ghouls" had physical models made, with CGI added for detail and animation. The T-60 power armor's flight system was achieved using practical effects using a [jet pack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daedalus_Flight_Pack "wikipedia:Daedalus Flight Pack") designed by [Gravity Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Browning "wikipedia:Richard Browning") and combined with a set of power armor costume legs. The scenes of Maximus landing in Filly and later losing control of his armor and flying away were all done by jetpack pilot , attached to a cable rig designed by stunt coordinators [Casey O'Neill](Casey%20ONeill.md "Casey O'Neill"), , and to avoid any accidental injury or damage. This marked the first time a jet pack was used for film production since *[Thunderball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thunderball "wikipedia:Thunderball")* in 1965.

Backgrounds for certain scenes such as the Vault 33 cornfield, the environments seen outside the Vertibirds, and the Los Angeles skyline seen from inside Griffith Observatory were filmed digitally using [the Volume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/StageCraft "wikipedia:StageCraft"), a type of soundstage where the set is surrounded by large LED panels, with Unreal Engine used to render the CGI backgrounds; this type of soundstage is said to provide better natural lighting than green screens. No such stage existed in New York prior to filming; the crew had a specialized Volume custom-made for them in Long Island for use in the show.

The title cards and credits animation sequences were created by production and design studio [Antibody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Clair "wikipedia:Patrick Clair"), which also produced similar videos for Jonathan Nolan's previous series *[Westworld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westworld "wikipedia:Westworld")*.

#### 

Main article: [Fallout TV series season 2](Fallout%20TV%20series%20season%202.md "Fallout TV series season 2")

For season 2, actress [Leslie Uggams](Leslie%20Uggams.md "Leslie Uggams"), who plays [Betty Pearson](Betty%20Pearson.md "Betty Pearson"), confirmed to *Screen Rant* in October 2024 that filming of the second season was set to begin the next month, in November 2024. On November 12, 2024, *Inverse* reported that filming had begun on the second season, taking place in Los Angeles. The moving of production to Los Angeles was a noted change from season one's filming locations in New York, Utah, and Namibia. A potential set construction for season 2 was spotted in [Baker, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baker,_California "wikipedia:Baker, California"), on November 23. It was at an abandoned motel where filming equipment was seen alongside a prop reconstruction of the "No Vacancy" sign from [Novac](Novac.md "Novac") in *Fallout: New Vegas*. On December 5, Ella Purnell and Walton Goggins were spotted filming scenes at a drive-in theater set near Kirk's Rock.

Although filming for season 2 was meant to resume in early January 2025, production was delayed for several weeks as a result of the [California wildfires that month](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_2025_Southern_California_wildfires "wikipedia:January 2025 Southern California wildfires"). In late January, a photo surfaced depicting a model of [Dinky the T-Rex](Dinky%20the%20T-Rex.md "Dinky the T-Rex")'s head from Novac being constructed.

In mid-to-late February 2025, a stretch of the disused [Valley Plaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley_Plaza "wikipedia:Valley Plaza") mall in North Hollywood was transformed into the [New Vegas Strip](New%20Vegas%20Strip.md "New Vegas Strip") (with portions visible to the public), with a disused movie theater becoming the exterior of [Gomorrah](Gomorrah.md "Gomorrah"), while new facades were built for the [Ultra-Luxe](Ultra-Luxe.md "Ultra-Luxe"), [Vault 21](Vault%2021.md "Vault 21"), [Michael Angelo's workshop](Michael%20Angelos%20workshop.md "Michael Angelo's workshop"), the [Lucky 38](Lucky%2038.md "Lucky 38"), and the [Fabulous New Vegas sign](Fabulous%20New%20Vegas%20sign.md "Fabulous New Vegas sign"), among other locations. In March 2025, more leaks confirmed the appearance of deathclaws, as well as a suit of NCR power armor, in a scene set outside the Lucky 38. Other leaks around that time showed a seemingly pre-War version of the same set, with the Lucky 38's signboard appearing to advertise an "American economic forum" sponsored by RobCo.

In mid-April 2025, photos surfaced on Reddit of interior film sets in [downtown Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Los_Angeles "wikipedia:Downtown Los Angeles") representing pre-War [McCarran International Airport](Camp%20McCarran.md "Camp McCarran"), including advertisements featuring Cooper Howard as [Vault Boy](Vault%20Boy.md "Vault Boy"), the [Silver Rush Gambling Hall](Silver%20Rush.md "Silver Rush"), [Trans World Airlines](Trans%20World%20Airlines.md "Trans World Airlines"), [Robert House](Robert%20House.md "Robert House") and the Lucky 38, and a reference to [Vault 21](Vault%2021.md "Vault 21"). These photos have since been removed on copyright grounds, but were reported on by some media sources. Around the same time, photos also surfaced on Reddit depicting members of [Caesar's Legion](Caesars%20Legion.md "Caesar's Legion") and NCR soldiers.

In late April 2025, Kyle MacLachlan stated that the filming for his scenes had concluded. He also expressed hope that the season would be able to release by the end of the year. All filming on the season was finished on May 8, 2025.

### 

In October 2022, a Bethesda Softworks video, "Fallout Retrospective - Beyond the Game," featured an interview with Jonathan Nolan in which he appeared to be on set for the TV series, with ruins in the background in a desert-like area, and being handed a [Nuka-Cola](Nuka-Cola.md "Nuka-Cola") by a person in [power armor](Power%20armor.md "Power armor"). That same month, Amazon Prime Video released a "first look" image from the series, revealing the existence of [Vault 33](Vault%2033.md "Vault 33"). During a November 2022 interview with [Lex Fridman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lex_Fridman "wikipedia:Lex Fridman"), Todd Howard confirmed that the series would not be a retelling of one of the game's plots; rather, it will actually be a new, unique story in the *Fallout* universe.

In August 2023, Amazon Prime Video confirmed the series will premiere in 2024. The new location of Vault 33 was also announced as being situated in [Los Angeles](Boneyard.md "Boneyard"). A teaser video was also shown by Todd Howard at Gamescom 2023. A more specific release date of April 12, 2024, was confirmed and announced on October 23, 2023. The announcement of the release date coincided with a new press release that included more casting details.

On November 28, 2023, a "first look" preview of the series was released in *Vanity Fair*, featuring significant, previously-unreleased plot details and new images. A display for the *Fallout* TV series was also hosted at [CCXP 2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CCXP "wikipedia:CCXP") in São Paulo, Brazil, from November 30 to December 3, 2023, during which Jonathan Nolan and Graham Wagner, as well as stars Walton Goggins, Ella Purnell, and Aaron Moten were available for interview. The first [official teaser trailer](Fallout%20-%20Teaser%20Trailer.md "Fallout - Teaser Trailer") for the series released on December 2, 2023, following a one-hour long "[Special Live Report](Fallout%20-%20A%20Special%20LIVE%20Report%20from%20Galaxy%20News.md "Fallout - A Special LIVE Report from Galaxy News")" with [Jon Daly](Jon%20Daly.md "Jon Daly") playing a [Galaxy News](Galaxy%20News%20Network.md "Galaxy News Network") reporter covering an exclusive unveiling of Vault 33. Another full-length trailer was released on March 7, 2024. The same trailer also revealed that the release date had been adjusted to April 11, premiering a day earlier, and that all episodes would arrive on that date.

On April 8, 2024, it was reported that *Fallout* had been renewed for a second season, with the state of California awarding tax incentives for several TV series (including *Fallout*) to film in the state. Of the series that had been awarded, *Fallout* had the largest budget, with $153 million in qualified expenditures for a second season. Its renewal came three days before the series' first season premiered. The same day, it was announced that the series release date had again been pushed up, now releasing another day earlier on April 10, at 6 p.m. Pacific Time. *Fallout* released worldwide on April 10, 2024. A second season was officially announced on social media on April 18, 2024.

## 

The *Fallout* show received generally positive reception, earning a 8.5 out of 10 based on over 100,000 votes on IMDb. It was Amazon Prime's most watched show since 2022's *[The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power](https://lotr.fandom.com/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Rings_of_Power "w:c:lotr:The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power")*. The show had 65 million viewers globally over its first 16 days. 60% of the show's audience was from outside the United States, with outsized performance in the UK, France, and Brazil. In October 2024, it was announced that the series had reached 100 million viewers worldwide.

At the [76th Primetime Creative Arts Emmy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/76th_Primetime_Creative_Arts_Emmy_Awards "wikipedia:76th Primetime Creative Arts Emmy Awards"), the series won Emmys for Outstanding Music Supervision (Trygge Toven, for the episode "[The End](The%20End%20episode.md "The End (episode)")") and Outstanding Emerging Media Program (for the interactive activation event *Fallout: Vault 33*). At [The Game Awards 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Game_Awards_2024 "wikipedia:The Game Awards 2024"), it won the award for Best Adaptation.

* In the episode "[The Trap](The%20Trap.md "The Trap")," a chalkboard depicting a timeline led to some viewers misinterpreting its presentation as Shady Sands having been nuked in 2277, before the events of *Fallout: New Vegas*, which would contradict that game. However, a week after the show was released, Todd Howard officially confirmed in an interview that Shady Sands was nuked after *Fallout: New Vegas*, clarifying the intention behind the chalkboard and erasing any potential lore discrepancies.
  + [Joshua Sawyer](Joshua%20Sawyer.md "Joshua Sawyer"), the lead designer of *Fallout: New Vegas*, said he does not care what the show does with *New Vegas* lore, feeling he does not own any of it, and simply views himself as a guest who worked on a project owned by something else (i.e. Bethesda). He mentioned he "doesn't get attached" to the lore like some fans of the game do, and thinks people can get too invested over fiction in an unhealthy way.
  + The [script](The%20End%20script.md "The End script") for the first episode of the show, "The End," was made publicly available in August 2024. The script confirmed Maximus' age during the show to be 19, and his age in the Shady Sands flashback scenes to be 6; this implies that the destruction of Shady Sands occurred in 2283, further alleviating concerns regarding the timeline.
  + Jonathan Nolan jokingly referenced this controversy during his acceptance speech for Best Adaptation at The Game Awards in December 2024, saying thank you to *Fallout: New Vegas* fans for not "burning [his] house down." He also teased New Vegas's appearance in season 2 by saying that fans would be glad they did not do so.

* *[Fallout 76](Fallout%2076.md "Fallout 76")* has allowed players to download a free [Vault 33 jumpsuit](Vault%2033%20jumpsuit.md "Vault 33 jumpsuit") underarmor to promote the TV series. It was originally available between December 2-12, 2023, and was again made available from April 9 - May 2024. The Ghoul's outfit was released on April 11, 2024, as a [Fallout 1st](Fallout%201st.md "Fallout 1st") exclusive, while Lucy's backpack was also released in-game.
* The *[Fallout SPECIAL Anthology](Fallout%20SPECIAL%20Anthology.md "Fallout SPECIAL Anthology")* was released on April 11, 2024 (one day after the TV show's premiere), as a collection of seven of the main *Fallout* games. It was announced on February 19, 2024, in anticipation of the TV show's release, with pre-orders opening the same day.
* Calling the Vault-Tec phone number featured in the show will result in audio of a man screaming as if he is being tortured.
* The naming convention for the series' episodes, in which the titles are a relevant word preceded by "The" (such as "The Head" or "The Ghouls"), is similar to the naming convention used for the quests in the *[Fallout: New Vegas](Fallout%20New%20Vegas.md "Fallout: New Vegas")* [add-on](Fallout%20New%20Vegas%20add-ons.md "Fallout: New Vegas add-ons") *[Lonesome Road](Lonesome%20Road%20add-on.md "Lonesome Road (add-on)")*. "The End" in particular is the name of both [a main quest](The%20End%20quest.md "The End (quest)") of the add-on as well as [the first episode](The%20End%20episode.md "The End (episode)") of the TV series.
* [Timothy Cain](Timothy%20Cain.md "Timothy Cain"), the creator of the original *[Fallout](Fallout.md "Fallout")* game, said he enjoyed the TV series.
* [Chris Avellone](Chris%20Avellone.md "Chris Avellone"), a designer for *[Fallout 2](Fallout%202.md "Fallout 2")* and *[Fallout: New Vegas](Fallout%20New%20Vegas.md "Fallout: New Vegas")*, was critical of the show. While he found it entertaining to watch and congratulated Todd and the crew for managing to create a *Fallout* show, he said it exhibited poor craftmanship, felt uneven, and claimed it does not respect its own internal lore. He felt the confusing plot points should be developed instead of leaving fans to make theories. He said the original *Fallout* games were not about the evils of capitalism, and that he groaned, laughed and wept upon hearing Vault-Tec was willing to drop the first nuke. He disliked the [retcons](Retroactive%20continuity%20TV%20series.md "Retroactive continuity (TV series)") such as the unnamed serum which seems to turn people into ghouls and heal their injuries, plus ghouls having to take drugs to prevent going feral. He was confused why the Ghoul did not exploit the power armor flaw against Maximus, why Shady Sands has a working trolley system, why the Brotherhood does not seem to teach proper sex education, and why [Frederick Sinclair](Frederick%20Sinclair.md "Frederick Sinclair") was portrayed as evil when it was not Avellone's intention. He questioned CX404's role, feeling she was too ignored in the show, and wished there was more of a bond shown between Cooper and Roosevelt. He found the water chip failure nod to *Fallout* to be confusing and embarrassing, and wished modern *Fallout* media were not about trying to rescue missing family members. He wished Moldaver and Bud Askins were more fleshed out antagonists. While he acknowledges the plot points will be developed in Season 2, he ultimately gave Season 1 around a 4/5/6 out of 10.
