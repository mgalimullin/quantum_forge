# Vault 31

**Vault 31** is a [location](Fallout%20TV%20series%20locations.md "Fallout TV series locations") near [Los Angeles](Los%20Angeles.md "Los Angeles") in the [*Fallout* TV series](Fallout%20TV%20series.md "Fallout (TV series)").

## Contents

## 

As part of the [Three Vaults](Three%20Vaults.md "Three Vaults") built underneath [Santa Monica](Santa%20Monica.md "Santa Monica") in greater Los Angeles, Vault 31 is directly connected to two other Vaults, [Vault 32](Vault%2032.md "Vault 32") and [Vault 33](Vault%2033.md "Vault 33"), forming a tripartite society. Outwardly, this society is divided into three Vaults to serve as a buffer against threats in case one Vault is compromised while still being able to support one another through times of crisis. The residents of the other Vaults are told that Vault 31 is just like them and only differs in having higher-quality resources, education, and food.

In reality, the Three Vaults themselves are the focus of a [social experiment](/wiki/Vault#True_purpose "Vault") entitled "[Bud's Buds](Buds%20Buds.md "Bud's Buds")," devised by pre-[War](Great%20War.md "Great War") [Vault-Tec](Vault-Tec%20Corporation.md "Vault-Tec Corporation") sales executive [Bud Askins](Bud%20Askins.md "Bud Askins"). Bud sought to use the three Vaults to breed a population of loyal Vault-Tec employees who would then go on to establish a monopoly over the wastes after everyone on the surface had perished from nuclear war, accomplished by populating Vault 31 with a hand-picked group of junior Vault-Tec executives whom he trained who would then be [cryonically](Cryonics.md "Cryonics") frozen in the Vault. In contrast, the other two Vaults were populated with people with traits he found genetically desirable who would live normal lives within the fallout shelters.

Per Bud's design, once every thirty years, his junior executives would be sent out into the other two vaults to eventually become their [overseers](Overseer.md "Overseer") by means of an unexpected crisis and breed with each new generation of residents to create a lineage of "super managers" loyal to Vault-Tec, guiding their post-War society towards Vault-Tec's mission to "inherit the Earth after [wiping] the surface clean." Bud himself would act as Vault 31's overseer, living well into the post-nuclear era by having his brain preserved in a rudimentary robotic shell described as a "Brain-in-a-Roomba."

Ostensibly, the three Vaults regularly conduct intermarriage and inter-Vault trade every three years in a ceremony known as the , but for the "residents" of Vault 31, this is a ruse concocted to provide them with any necessary items and to enable the individuals cryonically frozen in Vault 31 to join the other vaults and accomplish Bud's directive without garnering suspicion. By the 2290s, all the overseers of Vaults 32 and 33 have been members of Bud's Buds, including [George Yaffe](George%20Yaffe.md "George Yaffe"), [Betty Pearson](Betty%20Pearson.md "Betty Pearson"), and [Hank MacLean](Hank%20MacLean.md "Hank MacLean"). To ensure their placement in the overseer's seat, the populated vaults often experience crisis situations that the candidate from 31 successfully solves to boost their campaign prospects; this trend is so well-known that both of the vaults have adopted an electoral slogan celebrating candidates from Vault 31.

## 

As [Norm MacLean](Norm%20MacLean.md "Norm MacLean") and [Chet](Chet%20TV%20series.md "Chet (TV series)") discovered during their investigation into the destruction of the original residents of [Vault 32](Vault%2032.md "Vault 32"), they were not killed by the raiders [Moldaver](Lee%20Moldaver.md "Lee Moldaver") led when she kidnapped Norm's father Hank in 2296. Instead, a few years before the raiders' invasion, the residents killed themselves upon devolving into warring factions after killing [Overseer Ian Jackson](Ian%20Jackson.md "Ian Jackson") and his staff, also taking the time to mark the walls of the Vault in blood with the words "WE KNOW THE TRUTH" and "DEATH TO MANAGEMENT," indicating they had uncovered the existence of "Bud's Buds," though the means by which this occurred are unclear.

Later, after [Betty Pearson](Betty%20Pearson.md "Betty Pearson") is elected as the new Vault 33 overseer in Hank's absence, she arranged for a select number of 33's residents to immigrate to Vault 32, which was cleaned up and all traces of its destruction removed, all done supposedly on the recommendation of the elite residents of Vault 31. After the reassignments went through, [Steph Harper](Steph%20Harper.md "Steph Harper"), another transplant from Vault 31, became the overseer of Vault 32 and its new residents. Eventually, Norm's suspicions drove him to hijack Betty's overseer access and communicate with the staff in Vault 31, tricking its overseer into thinking he's Betty and that "the mission" had failed. Norm alone entered Vault 31 and met its overseer, the disembodied Bud Askins.

Avoiding Bud's attempts to stop him, Norm ventured into Vault 31 and uncovered its true nature as a repository of pre-War Vault-Tec executives, including his father and Steph, listening to the Brain-in-a-Roomba's explanation of how "Bud's Buds" were going to reshape the wasteland for the betterment of mankind and of Vault-Tec. In spite of Norm's attempts to escape and warn his fellow Vault dwellers, Bud sealed him inside of the Vault and threatened to let him starve to death unless he took the option of entering his father's cryo chamber.

Around this time, the inter-Vault communications were shut off, possibly due to sabotage by Norm. Before this had happened, Bud had arranged with Betty to write off Norm's disappearance as an expected transfer to Vault 31. While trapped in the Vault, Norm sustained himself on various ration kits he found around the Vault and dripping water. Bud would continue to try and encourage Norm to freeze himself in his father's cryo chamber, or accept death from a built-in lethal injection syringe on Bud's robot body. However, when he pointed out Norm only had "two rational choices," Norm decided to choose an irrational one and broke Bud's syringe so he would have nothing to threaten him with before releasing many of the [Vault 31 dwellers](Vault%2031%20dweller.md "Vault 31 dweller") from the cryo chambers.

Norm further damaged Bud's robotic body and left him upside down in a bucket to render him inoperational. Faced with the newly-unfrozen Vault-Tec employees, he lied that it was time for , but that Bud had died, which sent the employees into panic. Norm soon took charge over the employees by passing himself off as Bud's successor, a member of Bud's "race of super-managers" who was tasked with overseeing Bud's final test for the employees: to find a way to escape Vault 31. Using their knowledge of the Vault, the employees managed to open a roof ventilation hatch and climb to the surface with Norm, arriving in the sand dunes not far from the [Santa Monica Pier](Santa%20Monica%20Pier.md "Santa Monica Pier").

## 

There are some minor inconsistencies in the layout of the Vault's cryo chambers within the episodes. During the first Vault 31 sequence in "[The Beginning](The%20Beginning.md "The Beginning")," there is one shot which focuses on a row of four empty cryo chambers, with the occupants of the first three chambers revealed to be Steph Harper, Betty Pearson, and Hank MacLean (in that order). However, all other wider shots of the Vault show that each section of a row only has three cryo chambers, not four, and in "[The Innovator](The%20Innovator.md "The Innovator")" and "[The Golden Rule](The%20Golden%20Rule.md "The Golden Rule")," Steph's chamber is missing from that row.
